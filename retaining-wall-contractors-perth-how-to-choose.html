A dependable choice begins with the block: coastal sand, Darling Scarp foothills, shallow soil over limestone, or clay-prone Swan Valley flats. Measure retained height, note access, boundaries and visible water, then match the brief to limestone, post-and-panel, besser block, timber sleeper, rock or boulder work.   • Define the job: garden edge, level pad, driveway approach or adjoining land.
  • Name constraints: sand, rock near the surface, clay, slope, tight access and drainage.
  • Set decision order: ground first, system second, quote last.

Questions that reveal a real scope

Ask questions that force the contractor to explain judgement, not recite a rate. A useful answer links soil, water, height and finish to a system, then states what would change the recommendation.

  • Which ground condition on this block drives the design: sand, shallow rock, clay, slope or boundary pressure?
  • Is the wall a low terrace, a structural pad support, driveway edge or replacement of failed work?
  • Which systems are ruled out here, and what site evidence would bring one back in?
  • Where will subsoil water go: agricultural pipe, gravel, discharge point and reinstatement around the face?
  • Who confirms the permit position before excavation, and which standard or compliance step applies?
  • What access limitation could alter plant, spoil handling, backfill or the finished wall face?

If identical answers come back for very different blocks, the scope is probably too generic to price confidently.

Approvals before allowances

Treat approval as part of the build conversation, not an add-on discovered later. In WA, permit exposure is generally referenced beyond 0.5 metres of retained ground, yet a lower wall can still become approval-sensitive where it connects to other work, affects adjoining land, sits on a boundary or forms part of a substantial dividing fence.

Raise AS 4678, the Certificate of Design Compliance pathway and local government confirmation where relevant. The homeowner should not act as certifier, but the written quote should name who checks the approval position before the job is treated as complete.

Compare quotes by exclusions

The indicative band is only the wall-face signal. It leaves out GST, excavation, drainage and difficult access, so two similar totals can hide different assumptions. Put every allowance beside the physical problem it solves.

  • Earthworks: excavation depth, spoil removal, backfill and tight-access plant.
  • Water control: subsoil drainage components, labour, discharge and reinstatement.
  • Compliance: engineering input, permit support and who confirms approval.
  • Finish: texture, colour, capping, corners and any boundary detail.

A cheaper metre rate with unstated exclusions can cost more than a higher rate where spoil handling, drainage and reinstatement are itemised. Comparable quotes share scope, site conditions and exclusions; the headline rate comes after that.

Who this fits, and who it does not

This angle suits Perth homeowners planning a new wall, replacing a failed one, comparing concrete systems with limestone or rock work, or finding that landscaping has become permit-sensitive through height, boundaries or adjoining land. It fits less neatly where someone wants one universal material ranking, because coastal sand plain jobs, foothill sites and alluvial flats ask different questions.

Use the method when the block can be described: retained height, suburb, slope impression, soil behaviour, access, nearby boundaries and whether new work, repair or replacement is needed.

  1. Measure and describe. Record retained height, suburb, slope, soil impression, access, nearby boundaries and whether the job is new, repair or replacement.
  2. Narrow systems with reasons. Ask which wall types are ruled in or out for the block, and what evidence would change that call.
  3. Check approval early. Raise the 0.5 metre reference point, boundary triggers, AS 4678 and who confirms compliance.
  4. Compare written exclusions. Match GST, excavation, drainage, access, engineering and reinstatement line items before comparing totals.

Common questions

Is the $250 to $600 per square metre figure a fixed Perth rate? No. It is an indicative installed wall-face range. GST, excavation, drainage and difficult access sit outside that band, so totals depend on scope and site conditions.

Can a low garden wall still need approval? Yes. Even a wall below the commonly referenced 0.5 metre trigger can require approval where it connects to other building work, affects adjoining land, sits on a boundary or forms part of a substantial dividing fence.

What makes two quotes comparable? They share the same retained height, ground description, drainage plan, access assumptions, finish, compliance responsibilities and written exclusions before any metre rate is compared.
